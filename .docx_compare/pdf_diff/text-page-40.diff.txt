+8 Conclusion and Future Work
+8.1 Conclusion
+This work brings together an integrated desktop platform for taking in one URDF robot file and 
+outputting one locomotion task in natural language, and for training RL policies directly 
+without having to write low level simulator scripts. Each of the five main goals listed in 
+Chapter 1 were met.
+It involves autonomously extracting motion physics from the URDF tree, using NVIDIA Isaac 
+Sim simulator on GPU hardware for training, PyBullet for parameter inspection and a Flutter 
+frontend, and a FastAPI-orchestrated backend. Rewards are expressed using a limited set of 
+vocabulary (assessed math expressions are validated) and an LLM (under user validation 
+process). All the application was analysed using the Isaac Sim running 34 times with four 
+different families of robots and three different continuous control algorithms.
+There were three significant outcomes of the experimental trials. In the case of multi-platform 
+learning, the CPU was eliminated as the learning process' bottleneck, and parallel execution 
+on the GPU was used, where the maximum throughput at the single workstation level with the 
+RTX 5000 GPU was 32,056.8 environment steps per second. The impact of learner selection 
+was at best weakly significant for quadrupedal models such as the Go1 and all three models 
+(PPO, SAC, TD3) achieved a high number of survival episodes with over ninety six percent and 
+converged to similar reward scores. Conversely, algorithm choice was a direct contributor to 
+the training viability of more dimensional platforms, like the Valkyrie humanoid. Among the 
+three methods, only SAC maintained the stable balance throughout the entire simulation and 
+resulted in an eighty two point one percent survival rate, whereas PPO and TD3 had a 
+maximum of twenty percent survival rates but then became numerically unstable at later 
+stages.
+The iterative integration was mediated by means of failure, as the engineering process was 
+motivated primarily in this way. At first, it was necessary to introduce the robot relative floor 
+falling terminations and to give up fragile rule based naming, in favour of a more powerful 
+traversal mechanism of kinematic tree. The galloping traversal conditions led to the need for 
+explicit foot surface contact states modelling in Isaac Sim; Directories and safety issues 
+around arbitrary Python execution necessitated the need for an AST expression compiler.
+One note was that TD3 was not sufficiently "rewarded" with the Go1 galloping test and so it 
+was emulating a fast trot gait. This also received an unwanted locomotion mode, and the 
+platform failed to reach required height of airtime during the locomotion phase, which was 
+also given very high scalar reward. As demonstrated in this experiment, unconstrained total 
+reward volumes are not sufficient on their own to indicate the actual movement behaviour – if 
+it is not monitored using contact signals and episode length and seen from the preliminary 
+videos, it is of little consequence. Lastly, although the internal AI assistant was able to 
+sometimes successfully produce functional reward functions from natural language input, it 
+still needs a much larger validation set to ensure it is robust in complex engineering 
+situations.
